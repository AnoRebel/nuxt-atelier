---
title: Drafts and publishing
description: Work on a page without visitors seeing it, publish when it's ready, schedule it, share a preview, and go back to an earlier version.
---

Some kinds of content on your site have **drafts**: what you save isn't on the site until you publish it. Your developer decides which kinds work this way. The rest go live as soon as you save.

## Saving and publishing

While you edit, save as often as you like. Saving changes the draft only; visitors keep seeing the version that was last published.

When it's ready, **publish**. Everything in the draft goes live at once: the text, the blocks, and the page's address if you changed it. If the address changed, visitors who use the old one are sent to the new one.

A page with changes that aren't published yet says so, so you can tell a draft in progress from one that's live.

You need permission to publish. Without it you can still write and save drafts, for someone who can publish to look over.

## Taking a page off the site

**Unpublish** removes a page from the site: its address shows "page not found", and it isn't redirected anywhere. The draft stays, so you can publish it again later.

## Publishing at a set time

Instead of publishing now, you can **schedule** it for a date and time. Schedule a sale page for Monday at 9:00 and it goes live then, even if nobody is working. You can also schedule a page to be taken off the site, say when the sale ends.

A schedule publishes the page as it was when you scheduled it. If you keep editing afterwards, those changes stay in the draft and aren't published with it, and the page says which version is scheduled. To publish your latest changes at that time instead, schedule it again.

Times are your own time zone's. If something stops a scheduled publish, for example another page has taken its address meanwhile, the schedule shows it failed and why.

## Showing a draft to someone

To see your draft as it will look, on its real address, use **preview**.

To show it to someone who doesn't sign in, like a client, create a **preview link**. It opens that one page's draft, and stops working after the time you choose: a day by default, at most 30 days. After that, it shows a message that the link has expired, and none of the draft.

## Earlier versions

Every save and every publish is kept in the page's **history**: who saved it, when, and whether it was published. Open **History** below the form to see them, newest first. Tick two versions to compare them and see what changed: text, fields, and blocks added, removed, moved or edited.

To go back, **restore** a version. It becomes your new draft, and the versions in between stay in the history, so nothing is lost. Publish the restored draft when you're happy with it.

Very old versions are cleaned up after a while. Your developer sets how many are kept; versions that were published are always kept.
