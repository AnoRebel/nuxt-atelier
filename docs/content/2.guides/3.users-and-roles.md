---
title: Users and roles
description: Who can do what in the CMS, and how to sign in safely.
---

Everyone who works in the CMS has their own account and one **role**. The role decides what they can see and change.

![The users list: an administrator and an editor, each with their role](/media/admin-users.webp)

## Roles

- **Administrator** can do everything, including managing users and roles. There's always at least one: the CMS won't let the last administrator lose the role or be deleted.
- **Editor** is created with the CMS: it can create, edit, publish and delete all content, and manage media.
- Your site may have others, such as an author who can only publish their own articles, or a reviewer who can't change some fields.

![A new role being made: what it manages, and what it may do with each content type](/media/admin-role.webp)

A role can allow, for each kind of content:

- creating, reading, editing, deleting and publishing;
- some of those only on entries you created yourself;
- hiding some fields, or showing them without letting you change them.

If you can edit something but not publish it, your changes are saved as a draft for someone who can publish.

A role change applies straight away: there's no need to sign out and in again.

## The first administrator

A new site has no accounts. The first person to open the admin creates the first administrator account, and is signed in. After that, new accounts are created by people who manage users.

## Signing in

You sign in with your email and password. After several wrong passwords, sign-in pauses for a while for that account, to stop anyone guessing it; wait and try again, or ask an administrator to reset your password.

Signing out ends your session on the server, not just in your browser. If an administrator resets your password, you're signed out on all your devices.

### Passkeys

If your site has passkeys turned on, you can add one after signing in with your password. Next time, sign in with your device's fingerprint, face or PIN instead of typing a password. Your password still works, as a way back in if you lose the device.

### Signing in with another account

Some sites let you sign in with an account you already have, such as GitHub or your company's. The first time, you may find you can't reach anything yet: an administrator needs to give your new account a role.

## The activity log

Administrators can see a log of who signed in (and failed to), and of changes to users, roles and passkeys: who made each one, when, and what changed.
