---
title: Page addresses
description: Where your pages live on the site, what happens when you rename one, and redirects.
---

Pages, posts and other content that appear on the site each have an **address**, like `/about` or `/blog/spring-sale`. You don't type it in: it's made from the entry's fields, usually its title or slug. Your developer chooses the pattern for each kind of content, such as `/blog/` followed by the slug.

## Every address is used once

Two entries can't share an address. If you save a page with a slug another page already uses, saving stops and tells you which entry has it. Pick another slug.

On a site in more than one language, an entry can have an address in each one, like `/projects/harbour` in English and `/projektid/harbour` in Estonian.

## Pages inside pages

Some kinds of content can sit under a parent. A page **Team** under **About** lives at `/about/team`. Move **About** to `/company` and everything under it moves too: **Team** is then at `/company/team`. A page can't be placed under itself, or under a page below it.

## Renaming a page

When you change something that makes up the address, the page moves to its new address. The old one keeps working: visitors and search engines that use it are sent on to the new one. That happens for every page under it as well, so no bookmark or search result breaks.

Links you made to a page in the editor point at the page itself, not at its address. They follow it when it's renamed, without you editing the pages that link to it. If you delete a page, links to it become plain text.

## Pages the site itself provides

A few addresses belong to pages your developer built, like a contact form. If you give an entry the same address, the site keeps showing the built page there. The CMS warns you when that happens, and shows which built page wins. Choose another address for the entry.

## Redirects

A redirect sends visitors from one address to another. Use one when you remove a page and want its visitors to land somewhere useful, or when an old address from a previous site should keep working. You need the redirects permission; editors have it.

Each redirect has:

- **From**: the old address, like `/old-offer`. `/blog/:slug` matches any single part in place of `:slug`, and a `*` at the end matches everything after it.
- **To**: an address on this site, like `/offers`, or a full address on another site, starting with `https://`. It can reuse the matched parts: `/blog/:slug` to `/news/:slug` keeps the slug.
- **Type**: permanent (301 or 308) when the old address won't come back, temporary (302 or 307) when it might.

The CMS refuses a redirect that would send visitors around in a circle, one from an address a page uses, and a second redirect from the same address. Redirects made automatically when you rename a page are marked as such; you can change or delete them like your own.
